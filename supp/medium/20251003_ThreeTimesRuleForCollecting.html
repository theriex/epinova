The Three Times Rule for Music Collecting

Building a collection of music you'll enjoy listening to for years takes consideration. Every piece of music in your library reflects you at a moment in time, but some pieces travel with you through time as a core part of your life. You won't know which it's going to be when you buy an album, but you can maximize your chances by listening to an album three times before you buy it.

Why it works

Your connection to any piece of music comes from your mood, what you hear, and how it relates to you personally. You explore these interactions when you listen to new music. The more you explore, the more likely you are to find music that will become part of your life forever.

Your first encounter with a piece of music is pretty loaded. You might notice a piece playing while you are doing something else, a friend whose musical appreciation you trust might have suggested something, you might be at a concert, or find yourself listening in some other situation. Whatever your listening situation is, it's not going to be the same as when you go into your personal music library to listen. Having a path from your first listen through to your personal library helps you collect well.

On your first listen, you might decide you must immediately purchase a recording for your permanent collection, or you might think it's probably worth listening to again, or you might recognize the music as impressive but not for you, or you might just consign what you are hearing to the endless stream of ephemeral sounds of the day. This is a complex decision and not entirely conscious, so it helps to have a technique you can use to maximize your chances of making good choices.

It's still ok to impulse buy. Impulse buys reflect your musical life journey and add experiences to your library. All of my impulse buys still hold at least some nostalgic or historical interest even after nearly half a century. Several continue in rotation to this day. That said, a collection of impulse buys might feel more like a travel log than a music home. Following an approach allowing consideration that is integrated with your listening and enjoyment lets you make more informed collection decisions.

How it works

The first time you listen to a piece of music, decide if you might want to listen to it again. That's all you need to do for the first step. You might be swayed if a friend told you to play something, but this is about you. It's not a heavy decision, if the possibility of a second listen has not automatically made it into your head, then the music has not made the cut. You can enjoy plenty of things you are hearing at the time without needing to repeat them. That's normal. If something does stand out, make a note of it. Identify the piece, and write a few descriptive words for consideration by future you.

On second listen you are anticipating music that is more remarkable than average, and you are prepared contextually from your previous descriptive words. When listening a second time, you simply need to add whatever reactions you have. Literally add "On 2nd listen, ..." to your note. You might have discovered something substantial, or it might have just been a good match for your mood at the time and stood out in the mix. At this point you are either going to continue to consider this piece of music for your personal library, or mark it as notable and drop it. Again, this is not a heavy decision. Keep considering if you want, otherwise punt.

The third time you listen should be a few weeks after the second time. First-listen-you thought it was a good idea at the time, and second-listen-you confirmed there was probably something to it. Listening now and taking these past reactions into consideration, you can probably guess if this music should go in your library. You might guess wrong, but you have a good chance.

So far, I have not regretted any purchase I've made using the 3x rule, and the consideration process has provided guidance and insight into my musical listening life journey. Over the decades, some of my library music resonates less with me, while other music in my collection has grown with me more than I would have imagined. Regardless of how it goes over time, my library is my musical home.

History

Applying the 3x rule back in the day was more organic. You would listen to the radio and if you heard something remarkable you would wait for the DJ to run down title and artist so you could make a note of it. That took more effort and memory, but the DJs at the cool stations were well informed tastemakers and the music was well curated. When you hung out with friends at their place, they would spin what they had and you would make a mental note of anything that particularly interested you. When you went looking at the record store, you could hear what they were playing and ask about things. Most people knew what they were listening to. You could even inquire about music playing at cafes, bars or shops.

Moving towards the turn of the century, music channels got increasingly premixed and optimized from big corporate top down. Discovery was more of a scramble. If you wanted to hear anything beyond the mainstream you had to actively follow specialized music sources like world and local newsletters. At one point I had my tape deck on record/pause while listening to radio so I could just press play when something struck me. I'm still trying to Shazam my way through that stuff and buy anything missing, but it fails about 10% of the time. My IUMA tracks made it through since they were already digital, even if not the best quality. Friends made mixes for each other, and direct artist purchases started to get more established.

When the all-you-can-eat music buffet arrived, everything you couldn't previously find or afford was as good as yours, and you could explore forever. Literally. It's still wonderful, but people are overwhelmed with content and trusted curation is rare, so I'm writing about this 3x rule as a core personal strategy in case it's helpful to others. This is not new, and plenty of people do this already, but it seems worth mentioning.

Making it work for you

Applying the Three Times Rule to your listening and collecting involves (you guessed it) three steps:

  1. When music you're hearing might be worth a second listen, save identifying info with a few impression words to remind yourself.
  2. When you revisit and listen again, note your 2nd listen impression and decide to keep considering or not.
  3. A few weeks later, listen a third time and consider what you are hearing together with your previous impressions. Decide to add to your personal music library.

You can follow the Three Times Rule any way you like. Sticky notes, email messages, notepad, list manager, task manager, web spreadsheet, whatever. You can use the diggerhub.com/bookmarks log entry page (beta) if you've got nothing better. As long as you are can find what you are considering, that's all you need. Best wishes building, improving, and expanding your musical home.
